# Chapter One

Mara Voss was twelve the autumn the letter came. She looked out over the harbor of Port Ash with her green eyes and wanted out. She was stubborn and she was loyal, and she was afraid of deep water, which on an island is a hard thing to be. She lived with her uncle, Elias Ward, the keeper of the light.

Elias had a scar on his chin and a limp he never explained. He was a quiet man who wanted only to keep the light burning, and fire was the one thing he feared. Anna, the cook, fed them both and said little. She was kind.

The tide here turned every nine hours. Nobody on the island thought that strange. The old war had ended twenty years ago, and the steam ferry still ran on Sundays, past oil lamps along the quay.

# Chapter Two

The storm came on the second night and sank the ferry in the harbor. Mara and Elias watched it go down from the rocks. Tom Reed, a sailor, waited at the dock for men who did not come back.

That same night Elias climbed the lighthouse, as he always did, up past its white walls. Port Ash was two hours on foot from the lighthouse, and he made the walk in the rain.

# Chapter Three

Mara found the letter in the wreckage. It was addressed to her mother. She carried it for three days before she could open it.

Annie sang in the kitchen while she cooked, loudly and badly. Mara wanted to tell her to stop. The light must burn, Elias said, whatever the letter says.

# Chapter Four

Mara read the letter at last. It said her mother was alive on the mainland. Tom Reed offered to take her across when the weather turned.

# Chapter Five

She left on the first calm morning. Elias stood on the rocks and did not wave. She wanted out, and now she was out, and the light burned behind her all the way to the mainland.
